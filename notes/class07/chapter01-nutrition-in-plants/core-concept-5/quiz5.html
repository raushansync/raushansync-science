After This Core Concept

Food production during photosynthesis is not an isolated event — it is the starting point for everything the plant body needs.

  • Some food is used immediately to release energy for growth and repair.
  • Excess food is stored as starch in leaves, stems, roots, fruits, or seeds.
  • Starch is preferred for storage because it is stable, insoluble, and reversible.
  • Carbohydrates can be converted into fats and, with nitrogen from the soil, into proteins.

This means the carbohydrate formed during photosynthesis is not a final product — it is a raw material that the plant uses to build its entire body.

Understanding what happens after food is made helps explain why starch is used as evidence for photosynthesis, and why plants can survive even when sunlight is not available.
