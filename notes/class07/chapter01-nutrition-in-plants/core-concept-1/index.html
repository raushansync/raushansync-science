a. Intuitive Explanation

Every living organism, whether it moves or stays fixed in one place, is constantly active on the inside.

Even when a plant looks still, many things are happening:

  • new cells are being made,
  • damaged parts are being repaired,
  • substances are being transported,
  • internal balance is being maintained.

All of this activity requires energy and building material.

Food is not just something that satisfies hunger. Food is the source of raw material and usable energy that keeps life running moment by moment.

Without a continuous supply of food, a living body cannot:

  • grow,
  • repair itself,
  • or even maintain its existing structure.

Life does not pause its needs.

b. Formal Idea

Food is required by all living organisms because it supplies:

  • energy to carry out life processes, and
  • materials needed to build, grow, and repair the body.

This is why food is considered a basic requirement of life, just like air and water.

This idea exists because living systems are not static objects. They are self-maintaining systems, and maintenance always consumes resources.

c. Deep Explanation

Food serves two inseparable roles in living organisms:

1. Food as a source of energy

Every activity inside a living organism—movement, transport, repair, response—requires energy.

This energy does not appear on its own. It is released gradually from food through internal processes.

Without food:

  • reactions slow down,
  • coordination fails,
  • and life processes stop.

2. Food as a source of matter

Living bodies are made of substances like carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals.

These substances:

  • form cell structures,
  • build tissues,
  • and replace worn-out parts.

Food supplies the raw materials needed to keep the body physically intact.

So food is not only “fuel”; it is also construction material.

This distinction matters because an organism may have energy for a short time but still fail to survive if it cannot rebuild itself.

d. Common Misconceptions

Misconception 1: Food is only needed when an organism is growing.

Why it feels correct: Growth is visible, so food seems linked only to increase in size.

Why it is incorrect: Even fully grown organisms constantly repair cells and maintain internal balance.

Correct way to think: Food is required for maintenance as long as life exists, not only for growth.

Misconception 2: Plants need less food because they do not move.

Why it feels correct: Movement is an obvious sign of activity.

Why it is incorrect: Internal processes like transport, synthesis, and repair occur continuously in plants.

Correct way to think: Activity is not only external. Internal activity defines life.

e. Conceptual Connections

This idea prepares the ground for an important distinction later in the chapter:

  • Needing food is common to all living organisms.
  • How food is obtained is what differs.

Understanding that all organisms need food equally prevents the false idea that plants are somehow “less alive” than animals.

This directly leads to the next concept: different modes of nutrition.

Concept Checkpoints (Think, don’t answer)

  • If an organism stopped growing but stayed alive, would it still need food? Why?
  • Which role of food—energy or building material—do you think is more fundamental, or are they inseparable?
  • Can something be alive if it does not need food at all?
