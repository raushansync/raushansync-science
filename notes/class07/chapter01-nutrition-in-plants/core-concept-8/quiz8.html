After This Core Concept

This final core concept completes the full picture of plant nutrition — from how food is made to how the soil that supports it all is kept alive.

  • Plants continuously remove nutrients from the soil as they grow, and harvesting removes those nutrients from the field entirely.
  • Decomposers break down dead matter and return minerals back to the soil, recycling nutrients and preventing permanent loss.
  • Nitrogen-fixing bacteria restore usable nitrogen to the soil, naturally rebuilding what crops take away.
  • Farmers add manure, compost, and fertilisers to replenish nutrients faster than natural processes alone can manage.
  • Fertilisers do not create nutrients — they restore what the soil has already lost.
  • Soil fertility is not permanent; it depends on a continuous cycle of loss and replenishment.

Nutrition in plants is not an isolated event inside a single leaf. It is part of a closed loop — connecting air, soil, plants, microorganisms, and human activity into one system that must stay balanced to keep working.
