1. What Is Science?

Science is a way of understanding the world around us.

It is not just a subject in a book. Science begins when we notice something, ask questions, and try to find answers using careful thinking and observation.

In simple words: Science helps us understand how and why things happen.

2. Why Do We Study Science?

We study science because:

  • We are naturally curious.
  • We want to understand nature and daily life.
  • We want answers based on evidence, not guesswork.

Science helps us:

  • Understand plants, animals, and our body.
  • Explain natural events like rain, heat, and seasons.
  • Make better tools, machines, and medicines.
  • Think clearly and logically.

3. Science Is Everywhere

Science is not limited to laboratories. You can see science in the following places.

  • In the kitchen (cooking food).
  • On the playground (motion, balance, force).
  • In nature (plants growing, animals changing).
  • In the sky (Sun, Moon, stars).
  • In water (rain, ice, steam).

Every place where something happens can be studied using science.

Think about a place where science might be happening.

4. Science Begins with Curiosity

Curiosity means wanting to know more.

Examples:

  • Why does ice melt?
  • Why do plants need sunlight?
  • Why does a pen stop writing?
  • Why does water boil when heated?

Asking such questions is the first step of science.

A good science learner is someone who:

  • Observes carefully.
  • Asks "Why?" and "How?"
  • Is not afraid to be curious.

5. Observation: The First Step of Science

Observation means noticing things carefully using our senses.

We observe using:

  • Eyes: shape, color, size.
  • Ears: sound.
  • Nose: smell.
  • Skin: hot, cold, rough, smooth.

Examples:

  • A seed growing into a plant.
  • Ice changing into water.
  • A caterpillar turning into a butterfly.

Observation helps us identify problems and ask better questions.

6. Science Is About Asking Questions

After observing, we ask questions such as:

  • What is happening?
  • Why is it happening?
  • How does it happen?
  • What will happen if I change something?

These questions guide us toward answers. There are no bad questions in science, only unanswered ones.

7. Guessing Is Allowed in Science (But Carefully!)

In science, a guess is called a possible explanation.

Example: If a pen stops writing, we may guess:

  • The ink is finished.
  • The ink is dried.
  • The tip is blocked.

But science does not stop at guessing. Every guess must be tested.

8. The Scientific Method (Thinking Like a Scientist)

The scientific method is a step-by-step way to find answers.

Steps of the Scientific Method:

  1. Observe something interesting.
  2. Ask a question about it.
  3. Make a guess (possible answer).
  4. Test the guess by experiment or observation.
  5. Check the result.
  6. Improve the guess if needed.

This process may repeat many times.

9. Science in Daily Life

We all use science, even without realizing it. Examples:

  • A cook adjusts water while cooking.
  • A mechanic finds why a tyre is flat.
  • An electrician checks bulb and switch.
  • A student adjusts study methods after results.

Anyone who observes, thinks logically, and tests ideas is thinking like a scientist.

10. Science Is Always Growing

  • Science is not fixed.
  • New discoveries can change old ideas.
  • New questions arise from new answers.
  • Learning science is a continuous journey.
  • We may not know all answers now, and that is okay.

11. Science Is a Team Effort

Scientists:

  • Work together.
  • Share ideas.
  • Help each other.
  • Learn from mistakes.

Learning science with friends makes it easier, more fun, and more meaningful.

12. A Message to You

You do not need to memorise science. You need to:

  • Observe carefully.
  • Ask honest questions.
  • Think patiently.
  • Try to understand.

To be a wise person, learn to be a "why" person.
